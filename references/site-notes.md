## What most of them share

- White or cool light grey backgrounds. None uses cream.
- One strong brand colour plus a deep neutral.
- Square or nearly square buttons, often outlined, often uppercase.
- Real photography rather than illustration.
- Left-aligned headlines that state what the firm does.
- Content built from case studies, sectors, dated insights, named testimonials, careers and office addresses.

## Where they disagree

- Cambridge Consultants uses a centred headline, pill buttons and a technology-style image. L.E.K. uses a rounded media frame. Treat these as exceptions, not the rule.
- Ekimetrics colours one phrase of its headline. Use that sparingly, and never with an italic serif word in a sans headline.

## What the second batch changes

This batch breaks several rules drawn from the first ten. Kin&Co uses a serif for key words inside a sans headline, and 4C uses a cream background with violet accents and pill buttons. Both of those are on the skill's avoid list. Innecto and Kin&Co centre their headlines. These are deliberate brand systems, not AI output, so the skill's rules may need to become "use with restraint and a reason" rather than outright bans. Decide after the non-consulting examples arrive.

## What attraction sites share

- Booking is the main action and is always visible: a high-contrast button in the header, often repeated in a top bar with an incentive such as a saving or "from" price.
- A top utility bar with today's opening hours, address or getting-there link.
- A full-bleed photo or video of the actual place or animals, never generic imagery.
- Type and colour come from the place's identity: serif and deep green, teal or red for stately homes, chunky rounded or cartoon display type and primary colours for family parks.
- Warm cream and off-white backgrounds are common and look right here, which confirms that "no cream" is a context rule, not a universal one.
- Seasonal and event content dominates the homepage: autumn, Christmas, what's on, weddings, stays and shops.

## Two sub-styles

1. Heritage (Chatsworth, Blenheim, Hever, Leeds Castle, Beaulieu): serif or restrained sans, deep heritage colours, calm layout, one strong booking colour.
2. Family adventure (Longleat, Woburn, Diggerland): loud display type, bright primaries, busy promotional layout, prices and offers up front.
