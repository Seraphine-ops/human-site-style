# Writing rules

Every word on the page follows these: headings, body copy, buttons, alt text, page titles and meta descriptions.

## Write like the business talks

- Say what's there. "Open 10am to 5pm, daily until 31 October" beats "Plan your perfect day out".
- Use the business's own words for things. If they call it the pig barn, don't call it an "immersive animal experience".
- Short sentences, plain verbs: is, has, does, makes. Not "serves as", "boasts", "features" or "represents".
- Sentence case for headings. "What's on this autumn", not "What's On This Autumn".
- Button labels say what happens: "Book tickets", "Call us", "See prices". Not "Get started", "Learn more" or "Discover".
- Prices, times, distances and names wherever they exist. If they're missing, leave a marked gap and tell the user. Never make them up.

## Punctuation

- No em dashes. None. Use a full stop, comma, colon or brackets.
- No en dashes or double hyphens as a substitute. Use "to" for ranges: "10am to 5pm".
- No arrows (→) in text or on buttons.
- No emoji.
- Exclamation marks: at most one on a page, and only if the business would really say it.
- No bold labels at the start of every line ("**Parking:** ..."). A short heading or a plain sentence does the job.

## Words to avoid

These show up far more in AI text than in anything people write. Use a plainer word, or cut the sentence.

delve, tapestry, testament, landscape (unless it's actual land), pivotal, crucial, vital, vibrant, intricate, meticulous, underscore, showcase, foster, highlight (as a verb), enhance, bolster, garner, elevate, unlock, unleash, empower, supercharge, streamline, transform, revolutionise, harness, leverage, utilise, facilitate, encompass, navigate (unless it's directions), embark, journey (unless it's travel), realm, seamless, robust, comprehensive, holistic, multifaceted, nuanced, paramount, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, game-changing, next-level, world-class, unparalleled, unprecedented, transformative, remarkable, stunning, breathtaking, profound, enduring, bustling, nestled, renowned, rich (as in "rich heritage"), hidden gem, must-visit, curated, bespoke (unless it's literally made to order), passionate, dedicated, synergy, endeavour, additionally, furthermore, moreover, notably, indeed.

Filler words to cut when the sentence works without them: really, truly, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually, deeply, fundamentally, incredibly.

## Phrases and patterns to avoid

- "Not just X, it's Y" and every version of it: "It's not X. It's Y.", "more than just a", "not only X but also Y".
- Scene-setting openers: "In today's fast-paced world", "When it comes to", "Nestled in the heart of", "Welcome to the world of".
- "Whether you're a X or a Y".
- "From X to Y" when it isn't a real range ("from families to foodies").
- "Look no further", "This is where X comes in", "Say goodbye to", "Where X meets Y", "X, reimagined".
- "Something for everyone", "memories that last a lifetime", "an experience like no other", "the perfect day out".
- "It's worth noting", "At the end of the day", "The truth is", "Make no mistake".
- Sentences that end in a vague -ing phrase: "..., making it the ideal destination".
- Vague claims of popularity or authority: "loved by thousands", "as featured in" without the actual feature.
- Lists of three by habit. Two items or four are fine. Use three only when there really are three.
- Every sentence the same length, or a run of short punchy fragments ("Fresh air. Real animals. Pure fun.").
- Closing lines that sum up what the section already said.
- A first sentence that repeats the heading.
- Fake urgency: "Don't miss out", "Limited time only", unless there really is a deadline, stated with its date.

## Check before finishing

Search the page text for "—", "–", "→" and every word in the list above. Read each heading and button out loud and ask whether someone who works at the business would say it that way. Rewrite anything they wouldn't.
