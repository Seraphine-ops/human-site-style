# Human site style

Build sites for small businesses and organisations that look like the business made them: specific, confident and content-led. Section 2 is the baseline for every site. Section 4 adds a style for the type of site, which adjusts the baseline where it says so. Section 3 always applies and no style overrides it.

The reference sites, with fonts and colours read from their live pages, are in references/site-notes.md. Read the notes for the chosen style before designing.

## 1. Brief before building

Do not start on layout until you can answer these. Ask the user for anything missing.

- What does the organisation do, in one plain sentence?
- Which style fits: consultancy or professional service, heritage attraction, or family attraction? If none fits, use the baseline alone.
- What is the one action that matters most: an enquiry, a booking, a ticket sale?
- Who is the visitor, and what do they need to know first?
- What real content exists: photos of the actual people or place, case studies, named testimonials, events, prices, opening times, addresses?
- What is the existing brand: logo, colours, typefaces? Build from it. If there is none, choose from the guidance for the style.

Then write one sentence stating the design direction, and keep every later choice consistent with it.

## 2. Baseline principles

These come from established consultancies and are the safe default for any site.

1. **Say what you do, plainly.** The hero headline describes the organisation or makes one clear, concrete claim. No vague slogans or word-play.
2. **Left-align headlines and body text.** Centre only short statements, if at all.
3. **One brand colour, used with restraint.** Pair it with a deep neutral such as navy, charcoal or dark green. The brand colour marks buttons, links, rules and small accents, and is never a gradient.
4. **White or cool light grey page background.** Use white or a grey such as #f4f4f4. Cream or warm off-white may be used inside the page for a section band, panel, card or footer.
5. **One or two typefaces with character.** Either one sans family throughout, or a serif for headlines with a sans for body. Headlines are large and usually regular or medium weight. Choose fonts that fit the brand, not ones that are currently fashionable.
6. **Square corners.** Buttons, images and panels use 0 to 4px radius. Buttons are solid or 1px outlined rectangles. Uppercase, lightly spaced button labels are fine.
7. **Real photography of the actual people and places.** Large, often full-bleed or offset beside text. Tint or desaturate toward the palette if needed. No 3D renders, abstract blobs, illustrations or decorative icon grids as the main imagery.
8. **Structure the page around real content.** Each section exists because there is something real to put in it, and has a plain heading.
9. **Proof over adjectives.** Concrete titles, testimonials with a name and role, and figures only when real, each labelled with what it counts.
10. **Little decoration, little motion.** A thin rule in the brand colour or a clipped panel corner is enough. A slow carousel or subtle fade is acceptable. Nothing bounces, glows or follows the cursor.

## 3. Hard rules

These apply to every site and every style.

- **No pill buttons.** No pill-shaped or fully rounded buttons anywhere, including navigation, tags, booking buttons and form or cookie controls.
- **No cream page background.** Cream is allowed only inside the page, never as the overall background.
- **No invented content.** No made-up figures, testimonials, client logos, prices, opening times or events. Use a clearly marked placeholder and tell the user what is missing.
- **No gradients** on backgrounds, text or buttons.
- **No em dashes.** Not in headings, copy, button labels, alt text or metadata. Use a full stop, comma, colon or brackets instead.
- **No AI-sounding copy.** Follow references/writing.md for every word on the page.

## 4. Styles

### Consultancy and professional services

Use the baseline as written.

- Typical page order: hero, what we do, featured case studies, sectors and services, insights with dates, careers, contact with office addresses.
- Case study titles state the outcome. Insights carry a date and a sector label.
- Keep the tone sober. Colour appears mainly in buttons, rules and one or two section bands.

### Visitor attractions (applies to both heritage and family)

These add to the baseline for any place people visit.

- **Booking is always visible.** A high-contrast "Book tickets" button sits in the header on every page. It may be the one place a second, brighter colour is used.
- **A top utility bar** shows today's opening hours and the address or a getting-there link. Add a real online-booking incentive here if one exists.
- **The hero is the place itself:** a full-bleed photo or video of the actual house, grounds, animals or rides.
- **Seasonal and event content leads** after the hero: what's on now, upcoming events, then plan your visit.
- **Plan-your-visit information is easy to find:** prices, opening times, getting here, accessibility, facilities and food.
- **A practical icon row is allowed** for visitor information such as tickets, opening times and locations. Decorative feature-card grids are still not.
- Typical page order: utility bar, header with booking button, hero, what's on, highlights of the place, plan your visit, stay or venue hire if relevant, membership or shop, footer with address and contact.

### Heritage attractions

Stately homes, castles, palaces, gardens and museums. Adjusts the baseline and the visitor attraction rules.

- **Serif headings are the default,** in a classic face at regular weight, with a clean sans for body and labels.
- **Heritage colours:** deep green, teal, burgundy or navy as the neutral, with one warmer accent for booking. Cream sections suit this style well, still never as the page background.
- **A centred heading is allowed** for a short welcome or seasonal line over the hero. Body text stays left-aligned.
- **Calm, spacious layout** with fewer, larger sections. Let photography carry the page.
- Use the organisation's crest, script logo or heraldry if it has one. Do not invent one.

### Family attractions

Safari parks, theme parks, farms and activity centres. Adjusts the baseline and the visitor attraction rules.

- **Bold display type is allowed,** including chunky, condensed or rounded faces, often uppercase for headlines. Keep body text in a plain, readable sans.
- **Two or three bright colours are allowed** instead of one, taken from the brand, such as yellow, red, orange or sky blue, with a dark neutral for text.
- **Prices and offers go up front,** for example "Tickets from" with a real price, or a real saving for booking online.
- **A busier, more promotional layout is fine,** with more sections, event banners and a strong photo of people, especially children, enjoying the activity.
- Buttons are still square or nearly square. Playfulness comes from type, colour and photography, not rounded shapes.

## 5. Avoid

- Zoom, parallax or scale effects triggered by scrolling.
- Glows, coloured shadows and glassmorphism.
- A centred hero with a pill or badge above the headline.
- Three identical cards with an icon on top, or cards inside cards.
- Very rounded corners on panels, images or cards.
- A single italic serif word inside a sans headline.
- Inter, Geist, Space Grotesk or Instrument Serif as the main typeface.
- Emoji in copy, headings or buttons.
- Placeholder copy left in place, stock icon sets and default favicons.

## 6. Before finishing

View the page at desktop and phone widths, ideally in a browser with a screenshot. Check it against the hard rules, the baseline, the chosen style and the avoid list. Fix what fails, then check again. Tell the user which style you used, which reference-site traits you drew on, and any rule you broke on purpose and why. Never break a hard rule.
